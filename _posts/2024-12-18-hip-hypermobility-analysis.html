This post is a streamlined overview of the hip hypermobility study and points to the full report, project summary, and interactive materials.

Study Summary

This analysis evaluated whether psychosocial measures such as pain catastrophizing, pain interference, and pain self-efficacy were associated with hip hypermobility in a clinical cohort. Using regression modeling and multiple imputation, the strongest findings were that age and gender mattered most, while psychosocial scores showed little evidence of meaningful association with Beighton Score.

Key Takeaways

  • Age and gender were the strongest predictors of hypermobility in this cohort.
  • Psychosocial variables did not materially improve prediction in the primary models.
  • The project includes a fuller write-up, a project summary page, and an interactive Shiny app.
